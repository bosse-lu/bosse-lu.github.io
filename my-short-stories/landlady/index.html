My short stories · 4e · Difficult · B1

The Landlady

A short story by Roald Dahl (1959)

📄 The text is on your worksheet from class. It is protected by copyright, so it is not online. This page is your reading guide: it follows the line numbers on your sheet.

For each part: read the lines with the help of the difficult words, answer the questions, then check the answer.

Lines 1–60

1. Arriving in Bath

Difficult words

  • the porter = l'employé de la gare
  • fairly cheap = assez bon marché
  • lodgings = un logement
  • the Branch Manager = le directeur de l'agence
  • briskly = d'un pas vif, rapidement
  • the big shots = les grands patrons
  • the paint was peeling = la peinture s'écaillait
  • neglect = l'abandon, le manque d'entretien
  • a notice = un écriteau

Questions

  1. Where does Billy come from, and where does he arrive?
    Check

    He comes from London. He arrives in Bath, by train.

  2. What time is it? What is the weather like?
    Check

    It is about nine o'clock in the evening. It is very cold and windy.

  3. How old is Billy? Why is he in Bath?
    Check

    He is seventeen. He is there for his job: he must find a room, then report to the Branch Manager.

  4. What do the houses in the street look like?
    Check

    They are tall and identical. They were once beautiful, but now the paint is peeling: nobody takes care of them.

  5. What does Billy see in a window?
    Check

    A notice that says BED AND BREAKFAST, and a vase of yellow chrysanthemums.

Lines 60–125

2. The window and the bell

Difficult words

  • to peer = regarder attentivement
  • the hearth = l'âtre, la cheminée
  • a dachshund = un teckel
  • curled up asleep = endormi en boule
  • a parrot in a cage = un perroquet en cage
  • a boarding-house = une pension
  • frightened = effrayé
  • to stare at = fixer du regard
  • a jack-in-the-box = un diable à ressort

Questions

  1. Which animals does Billy see in the room?
    Check

    A little dachshund asleep in front of the fire, and a parrot in a cage.

  2. Why does he think it is a good place to stay?
    Check

    The room is warm and comfortable, and he thinks animals are a good sign.

  3. He decides to go to The Bell and Dragon. What strange thing happens?
    Check

    The words BED AND BREAKFAST seem to look at him like a big black eye. They force him to stay, and he rings the bell.

  4. How quickly does the door open? Why is it strange?
    Check

    At once, before he takes his finger off the bell, like a jack-in-the-box. Normally you wait at least half a minute.

Lines 125–200

3. The landlady

Difficult words

  • a landlady = une logeuse, une propriétaire
  • to charge = faire payer
  • five and sixpence = cinq shillings et six pence (très peu cher)
  • the threshold = le seuil de la porte
  • a walking-stick = une canne
  • a nest = un nid
  • slightly dotty = un peu folle
  • choosy = difficile, exigeante
  • pale lips = des lèvres pâles

Questions

  1. Describe the landlady: age, face, eyes.
    Check

    She is about forty-five or fifty. She has a round pink face and gentle blue eyes. She is very welcoming.

  2. How much is the room? What does Billy think of the price?
    Check

    Five and sixpence a night, with breakfast. It is fantastically cheap: less than half of what he wanted to pay.

  3. What does Billy notice in the hall?
    Check

    There are no other hats, coats, umbrellas or walking-sticks: nobody else is staying in the house.

  4. What kind of visitor does she say she waits for?
    Check

    She is choosy: she waits for a young gentleman who is “just exactly right”, like Billy.

Lines 200–300

4. The room and the guest-book

Difficult words

  • a water-bottle = une bouillotte
  • chilly = frais, froid
  • to unpack = défaire sa valise
  • the law of the land = la loi du pays
  • harmless = inoffensive
  • a guest-book = un registre des clients
  • an entry = une inscription
  • it rings a bell = ça me dit quelque chose
  • a tea-tray = un plateau à thé

Questions

  1. What name does the landlady call him? Is it correct?
    Check

    She calls him Mr Perkins. No: his name is Weaver.

  2. Why must he sign the book, according to her?
    Check

    She says it is the law of the land.

  3. What does Billy think of her?
    Check

    She is a bit strange (“off her rocker”), but harmless, kind and generous. Maybe she lost a son in the war.

  4. How many names are in the guest-book? Who are they?
    Check

    Only two: Christopher Mulholland from Cardiff and Gregory W. Temple from Bristol.

  5. Why are the names strange for Billy?
    Check

    They ring a bell: he thinks he has heard them before, maybe in the newspapers.

Lines 300–410

5. Tea for two

Difficult words

  • handsome = beau
  • a sigh = un soupir
  • to fuss about = s'affairer
  • a ginger biscuit = un biscuit au gingembre
  • an undergraduate = un étudiant
  • to sip = boire à petites gorgées
  • a whiff = une bouffée (d'odeur)
  • pickled walnuts = des noix au vinaigre
  • leather = le cuir

Questions

  1. How does she describe the two young men?
    Check

    Extraordinarily handsome, tall and young, “just exactly like you”.

  2. Look at the dates in the book. What does Billy notice?
    Check

    The last entry is more than two years old, and Mulholland's is more than three years old.

  3. What does the smell of the landlady remind him of?
    Check

    Pickled walnuts, new leather, or the corridors of a hospital.

  4. Where are Mr Mulholland and Mr Temple now, according to her?
    Check

    They never left: they are still in the house, on the third floor, both of them together.

🔍 Clue hunt

A suspense story leaves clues. Find at least five clues in the text that show the landlady is dangerous, then check.

Check my clues
  1. The door opens at once, like a jack-in-the-box: she was waiting.
  2. The room is very cheap, and it is all ready for him.
  3. There are no other hats or coats: there are no other guests.
  4. She is “choosy”: she only wants young men who are “just right”.
  5. Only two names in the guest-book, two or three years old, and the names were in the newspapers.
  6. She forgets his name: Perkins, Wilkins…
  7. The dog and the parrot never move.
  8. She smells like the corridors of a hospital.
  9. The two young men “never left”: they are still on the third floor.
